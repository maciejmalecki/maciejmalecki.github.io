## Summary

The current draft `genai/_drafts/one-year-with-agentic-ai.md` carries two distinct themes that have grown large enough to stand on their own: (A) a **remote-control setup** story — driving a mini PC from an Android phone, the robustness demands of a remote-only rig, and the full automation that makes phone-only work possible; and (B) a **one-year retrospective** — the evolution from vibe coding through plan-and-execute to skills, the economics of model choice, and a grading of last year's predictions. This plan splits the single draft into two separate articles. **Article A (remote control) is published first** and **owns the "Remote means fully automated" section** (the full-automation precondition, the RBT harness, verification, subagents and the permission allowlist), because full automation is framed as the precondition that makes remote control possible. **Article B (one-year summary) is published second** and focuses on the evolution narrative plus the predictions scorecard. The two articles **cross-link both ways**: Article A forward-references the coming retrospective; Article B back-links to the published remote-control piece.

## Rationale

The draft's own opening promises one thing — "the contrast … is what this post is about" (the remote-control contrast) — while more than half its body is a retrospective on the year's tooling evolution and a point-by-point grading of past predictions. Two theses in one post dilute both; splitting lets each run at its natural length and be published on its own schedule. Publishing the remote-control piece first is the author's call: it is the more concrete, self-contained story (hardware, robustness, the automation that underpins it) and does not depend on the retrospective, whereas the retrospective naturally refers back to the now-established remote setup.

The single genuine design decision — which article owns the "Remote means fully automated" section, since it is both the *precondition for* remote control and a *product of* the year's evolution — was resolved in favour of Article A: it makes the first-published article substantial and self-contained, and keeps Article B focused on evolution and predictions. Cross-linking both ways matches this blog's established linked-essay style (as with `the-rise-of-vibe-coding`, `prompts-and-agentic-ai`, `why-dont-i-feel-emotional`).

This plan changes **content only** — it creates and edits drafts under `genai/_drafts/` and does not touch build config, templates, or Claude Code assets. The post lifecycle (scaffolding the second draft, front-matter, quality gates, promotion to `_posts/`) is handled by the `post-utils` and `task-utils` skills, invoked as the steps below direct; this plan specifies *what* the two articles contain and *how* they cross-link, not the mechanical file operations.
